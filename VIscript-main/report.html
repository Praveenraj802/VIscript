Abstract Executive Summary

Modern commercial facilities and residential gated communities deploy dozens to hundreds of closed-circuit television (CCTV) cameras operating continuously (24 × 7), producing approximately 1,440 minutes of raw video per camera daily. Human physical security guards and facility managers experience severe cognitive fatigue within 20 to 30 minutes of continuous observation, rendering real-time human monitoring largely ineffective. Retrospective incident investigation is equally arduous, requiring security personnel to manually scrub through hours of low-information video footage. Furthermore, traditional computer vision solutions rely on rigid bounding-box object detectors (e.g., YOLO, SSD) that lack high-level semantic reasoning, or invasive facial recognition algorithms that violate data privacy legislations such as the Indian Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDP Act) 2023.

This project introduces VisualIQ (viscript), an enterprise-grade, privacy-compliant, software-as-a-service (SaaS) surveillance intelligence platform. VisualIQ interfaces with pre-installed IP cameras (Hikvision, Dahua, CP Plus, RTSP feeds) without requiring expensive proprietary edge hardware. The platform utilizes a dual-mode video ingestion engine combining uniform temporal decimation (0.2 FPS, 1 frame per 5 seconds) with dynamic OpenCV-based motion-burst extraction to capture abrupt transient events while suppressing static background redundancy by over 96%. Extracted frames are analyzed via a multi-provider Vision-Language Model (VLM) architecture using Google Gemini 2.5 Flash as the primary engine, backed by Groq Llama 4 Scout / Llama 3.2 Vision, Ollama Qwen3-VL, and an automated deterministic non-LLM heuristic fallback to guarantee zero service interruption.

Crucially, the analyzer incorporates a sliding temporal context window that supplies the preceding three historical events to each frame analysis, eliminating stateless perception and enabling reliable detection of time-dependent anomalies such as loitering (>5 minutes), perimeter intrusions, and escalating altercations. Synthetic narrative shift transcripts are generated and indexed within a vector space via ChromaDB and Supabase (PostgreSQL 16). Facility managers and residents interact with a conversational Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) interface built on React 19, Vite, and FastAPI, querying community footage in natural human language ("Were there any unfamiliar individuals or suspicious deliveries after 11:00 PM?") to receive sub-3-second contextual answers accompanied by exact timestamp citations. VisualIQ enforces strict privacy boundaries: zero facial identification, zero biometric vector storage, and ephemeral frame disposal post-synthesis.

Regulatory Compliance Note: VisualIQ is architected from the ground up to comply with the statutory provisions of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDP Act) 2023 (India). No facial biometric templates, embedding vectors, or identifiable facial landmark coordinates are captured, processed, or persisted. All frames are purged from storage immediately upon transcript extraction.

1. Introduction Chapter 1

1.1 Background and Motivation

Video surveillance infrastructure has experienced exponential growth globally over the past decade. High-definition Internet Protocol (IP) cameras and Digital Video Recorders (DVRs) supporting the Real-Time Streaming Protocol (RTSP) are installed ubiquitously across gated communities, residential townships, corporate campuses, logistics hubs, and retail centers. Despite this pervasive hardware deployment, contemporary surveillance operations remain overwhelmingly passive and reactive.

In the typical operational setup of an apartment complex or corporate facility, a security control room features a "video wall" displaying between 16 and 64 simultaneous camera tiles. A human operator is expected to observe these tiles to maintain situational awareness. Empirical studies in human factors engineering consistently demonstrate that human visual vigilance deteriorates sharply within 20 to 30 minutes of continuous monitoring. Subtle security breaches—such as an unauthorized person scaling a rear boundary wall, an unattended delivery parcel deposited in a common lobby, or an individual loitering near parked vehicles for extended durations—invariably escape human detection.

When an incident is reported retrospectively (e.g., vehicle theft, property damage, or unauthorized entry), security personnel must allocate hours or days to manually scrub through petabytes of unstructured video footage. The process is labor-intensive, error-prone, and delays emergency intervention.

Recent advances in Generative Artificial Intelligence and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have fundamentally transformed computer vision. Unlike legacy Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) that merely classify pre-trained bounding boxes (e.g., "person: 0.89", "car: 0.94"), VLMs comprehend scene semantics, complex spatial interactions, human behaviors, contextual anomalies, and temporal narratives. Combining VLMs with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and dense vector embeddings unlocks the capability to convert unstructured pixel streams into searchable, structured, textual intelligence.

1.2 Objectives

The core objective of VisualIQ is to architect, develop, and deploy an end-to-end autonomous surveillance intelligence platform that bridges raw video feeds and conversational semantic query systems. The concrete engineering objectives are:

  • Autonomous Ingestion & Temporal Decimation: Develop an intelligent ingestion pipeline that extracts keyframes using uniform temporal sampling (0.2 FPS) combined with pixel-difference motion-burst triggering, reducing data ingestion volume by 96% while retaining critical incident frames.
  • Context-Aware Semantic Vision Analysis: Formulate a structured vision analysis engine using Gemini 2.5 Flash and Llama 4 Scout that injects the preceding three temporal events into the VLM prompt, allowing the model to distinguish harmless transient movement from prolonged loitering (>5 minutes) and security anomalies.
  • Multi-Provider Resilience Cascade: Engineer a four-tier graceful degradation pipeline (Gemini 2.5 Flash → Groq Llama 4 Scout → Local Ollama Qwen3-VL → Deterministic Heuristic Synthesis) ensuring 100% operational availability even during cloud quota exhaustion or network disconnection.
  • Automated Surveillance Shift Synthesis: Synthesize timestamped, structured narrative logs representing surveillance periods (morning, afternoon, night) and automatically compute security threat scores (0 to 10) with automated anomaly alerts for scores exceeding 6.
  • Semantic RAG Intelligence: Store vector embeddings of surveillance transcripts within ChromaDB and Supabase PostgreSQL 16, providing natural language conversational querying with verifiable timestamp citations in under 3 seconds.
  • Regulatory DPDP Act 2023 Compliance: Design the system strictly around behavioral descriptions rather than facial biometrics, setting faces_visible: false, avoiding facial embedding storage, and enforcing ephemeral frame lifecycle management.

1.3 Scope and Target Audience

The functional scope of VisualIQ encompasses:

  • Integration with standard IP/CCTV cameras via file uploads (MP4, AVI, MOV) and live RTSP streaming via circular video buffer workers.
  • Structured event extraction, anomaly detection, automated incident alerting, and daily security executive digest generation.
  • Single-pane-of-glass operator dashboard featuring real-time anomaly banners, interactive chronological event timelines, multi-camera filtering, and a conversational RAG terminal.

The primary target audience includes:

  1. Residential Welfare Associations (RWAs) & Apartment Society Committees: Seeking automated night-shift surveillance, visitor tracking, and rapid incident verification.
  2. Commercial Real Estate & Property Managers: Requiring audit trails for facility maintenance, perimeter security, and delivery logging.
  3. Security Operations Center (SOC) Dispatchers: Requiring real-time threat prioritization rather than unassisted manual video observation.

2. Literature Survey Chapter 2

2.1 Introduction

Automated video surveillance has evolved across three major technological epochs:

  1. First Generation (1995–2012) — Classical Computer Vision: Characterized by background subtraction (Gaussian Mixture Models), optical flow (Lucas-Kanade), edge detection, and handcrafted feature descriptors (Haar, HOG).
  2. Second Generation (2012–2022) — Deep Learning Object Detection: Driven by Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), You Only Look Once (YOLO), Single Shot MultiBox Detector (SSD), and Two-Stage Detectors (Faster R-CNN).
  3. Third Generation (2023–Present) — Multimodal Foundation Models: Powered by Vision-Language Models (Gemini 2.5 Flash, Llama 3.2/4 Vision), zero-shot reasoning, open-vocabulary scene interpretation, and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).

2.2 Related Work

2.2.1 Classical Background Modeling and Motion Analysis

Early automated surveillance systems relied on Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM) for adaptive background subtraction (Stauffer and Grimson, 1999). While effective in controlled indoor environments with static lighting, these algorithms produce extreme false-alarm rates when deployed in outdoor surveillance due to swaying foliage, shadows, camera jitter, and illumination shifts. Furthermore, pixel-level motion analysis cannot decipher the semantic nature of an event; a stray dog running across a driveway generates identical motion vectors to an intruder scaling a perimeter fence.

2.2.2 Deep Object Detection and Tracking

The emergence of YOLO (Redmon et al., 2016) and ByteTrack (Zhang et al., 2022) enabled robust, real-time localization of persons, vehicles, and backpacks at 30+ frames per second. However, deep object detectors exhibit fundamental limitations in enterprise security:

  • Semantic Blindness: Object detectors output coordinate boxes and class probabilities. They cannot interpret interactions (e.g., whether a person near an automobile is unlocking their personal vehicle or testing handles to commit vehicle theft).
  • High Inference Cost for 24/7 Processing: Running deep CNNs continuously on high-resolution 4K feeds across 32 cameras requires prohibitive enterprise GPU clusters (NVIDIA A100/H100), rendering continuous processing cost-ineffective for residential gated communities.

2.2.3 Facial Recognition and Privacy Legislation

Systems integrating deep facial recognition (e.g., ArcFace, FaceNet) have historically been utilized for physical access control and surveillance. However, global regulatory landscapes have shifted drastically. The European Union Artificial Intelligence Act (EU AI Act) classifies remote real-time biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces as an unacceptable or high-risk AI practice. Similarly, the Indian Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDP Act) 2023 imposes stringent statutory penalties (up to ₹250 Crores) for non-consensual processing, storage, and profiling of biometric personal identifiers. Consequently, modern commercial surveillance architectures must maintain operational intelligence while operating in a strictly non-biometric, privacy-preserving regime.

2.2.4 Vision-Language Foundation Models and RAG

Foundation VLMs (Radford et al., 2021; Google Gemini Team, 2024; Meta AI, 2024) combine vision encoders (such as ViT) with Large Language Model autoregressive transformers. These models exhibit zero-shot reasoning across complex visual scenes. Concurrently, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Lewis et al., 2020) has proven successful in textual enterprise knowledge bases. Applying RAG to temporal surveillance video metadata represents an emerging frontier, transforming unstructured video streams into indexed vector databases queried via natural language.

2.3 Gaps Identified

A systematic review of current surveillance solutions exposes five critical engineering gaps:

Domain Gap Existing Limitation Impact on Operations
1. Compute & Storage Bottleneck Continuous 30 FPS inference or raw video streaming across 24 hours. Storage exhaustion within days; enterprise GPU cloud bills exceed society budgets.
2. Stateless Visual Perception Frames evaluated independently without historical temporal continuity. Cannot differentiate a 15-second pause from 10-minute suspicious loitering.
3. Cloud Provider Fragility Hardcoded single-provider dependencies (Gemini or OpenAI only). Rate limits (429) or network disconnects cause total surveillance blindness.
4. Biometric Privacy Exposure CCTV analytics harvesting face embeddings and personal biometric signatures. Direct violation of DPDP Act 2023 with potential statutory fines up to ₹250 Cr.
5. Inaccessible Video Retrieval Operators must scrub through timeline video sliders manually. Investigations take hours; questions like "Any strangers last night?" cannot be answered.

2.4 Problem Statement

Surveillance management in modern residential gated communities and commercial facilities is constrained by cognitive limitations in human manual monitoring, prohibitive computing costs associated with raw video inference, privacy risks under data protection laws, and an inability to search recorded video using plain human language.

There is an urgent requirement for an autonomous, privacy-preserving software platform that can ingest CCTV feeds, intelligently decimate visual data without losing critical incident details, extract structured factual narratives using temporal context-aware vision models, ensure high uptime via multi-provider failover, and index narrative events within a vector database to enable instantaneous natural language intelligence queries with verifiable timestamp citations.

2.5 Proposed Solution: VisualIQ

VisualIQ resolves these operational and technological challenges through a software-centric pipeline:

  1. Intelligent Dual-Mode Decimation: Ingests video files and RTSP feeds, applying uniform decimation (0.2 FPS = 1 frame every 5 seconds) combined with an OpenCV-based pixel-difference motion trigger (θmotion = 0.08) to capture rapid burst motions while eliminating 96% of redundant frames.
  2. Temporal Context Frame Analyzer: Passes each frame to Google Gemini 2.5 Flash alongside a rolling memory of the previous three events. This enables the model to accurately detect behavior over time (such as loitering >5 minutes, forced entries, and escalating altercations) while strictly setting faces_visible: false to ensure compliance with the DPDP Act 2023.
  3. Four-Tier Resilience Engine: Implements a cascading failover architecture:
    Gemini 2.5 Flash → Groq Llama 4 Scout → Ollama Qwen3-VL → Deterministic Heuristic Synthesis
    This ensures zero service disruption during cloud API throttling or offline edge operation.
  4. Automated Structured Synthesis & Alerting: Combines raw frame events into comprehensive shift narratives (Morning, Afternoon, Night) and auto-flags anomalies with threat scores exceeding 6/10.
  5. Conversational Vector RAG Interface: Indexes synthesized surveillance narratives into ChromaDB (384-dimensional cosine similarity) and Supabase (PostgreSQL 16), enabling administrators to execute plain-English conversational queries on a React 19 dashboard and receive answers with precise timestamp citations in under 3 seconds.

2.6 Summary

Chapter 2 reviewed the transition from classical computer vision to contemporary multimodal vision-language architectures. The five critical domain gaps—compute waste, stateless frame analysis, cloud API fragility, biometric privacy exposure, and lack of semantic querying—were identified. VisualIQ resolves these limitations through intelligent dual-mode sampling, temporal context injection, resilient multi-provider failover, strict DPDP Act 2023 privacy preservation, and conversational vector RAG intelligence.

The purpose of this document is to specify the complete functional, non-functional, interface, and architectural requirements for the VisualIQ Surveillance Intelligence Platform (viscript). This specification is intended for software engineers, systems architects, security operations personnel, and academic evaluators.

3.1.2 Scope

VisualIQ is a software-as-a-service (SaaS) application that interfaces with existing IP/CCTV surveillance hardware. The platform provides:

  • Video ingestion supporting MP4, AVI, MOV formats and RTSP live streams.

3.2 General Description

3.2.1 Product Perspective

VisualIQ operates as an intelligent software layer between existing CCTV hardware and security management staff. It connects upstream to standard DVRs/NVRs or video file uploads, and downstream to cloud vector databases, multimodal LLM providers, and client web browsers.

3.2.2 Product Functions

  • Video Ingestion: Accepts uploaded video files and connects to RTSP camera feeds.
  • Dual-Mode Frame Extraction: Performs uniform temporal decimation (0.2 FPS) and motion-burst triggering.
  • Temporal Context Analysis: Analyzes frames using Gemini 2.5 Flash and Llama 4 Scout while retaining a rolling window of recent events.
  • Anomaly Scoring & Alerting: Generates numerical threat scores (0 to 10) and dispatches immediate anomaly notifications for scores >6.
  • Narrative Synthesis: Converts frame events into cohesive, shift-based surveillance narratives.
  • Vector Indexing & Storage: Stores vector embeddings of transcripts in ChromaDB and metadata in Supabase PostgreSQL.
  • Conversational RAG Q&A: Answers user queries in plain English with timestamp citations.
  • Executive Daily Digest: Generates comprehensive daily security summary reports.

3.2.3 User Characteristics

  1. Facility / Estate Security Manager: Non-technical domain expert who oversees perimeter security, investigates reported incidents, and manages daily staff briefings.
  2. Society Committee / RWA Member: Administrative user who reviews daily security digests and validates service quality.
  3. Security Operations Center (SOC) Guard: Primary end-user monitoring the real-time alert feed, acknowledging and resolving system-flagged anomalies.
  4. System Administrator / DevOps Engineer: Technical user responsible for configuring API keys, managing camera RTSP URLs, and monitoring system health.

3.2.4 General Constraints

  • Privacy Compliance: Under the DPDP Act 2023, the platform must never perform facial recognition or store biometric embeddings.
  • Bandwidth & Storage: Raw video frames must be discarded after narrative synthesis to prevent local storage exhaustion.
  • Network Latency: Cloud VLM latency must remain within acceptable bounds, and local fallback paths must activate if external latency exceeds 10 seconds.
  • Browser Compatibility: The user interface must support all modern evergreen web browsers (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari) without requiring external plugins.

3.2.5 Assumptions and Dependencies

  • Video feeds have a minimum resolution of 720p (1280 × 720) at 15 FPS to ensure adequate visual clarity for VLM object identification.
  • Outbound HTTPS connectivity on port 443 is available for Google Gemini and Groq cloud APIs.
  • Redis server (version 6.0+) and PostgreSQL (version 15+) or Supabase managed cloud instances are available and reachable.

  • NFR-1: Performance & Latency: Conversational RAG queries must resolve and return in under 3.0 seconds under nominal network conditions. Visual frame inference must not exceed 1.2 seconds per frame.
  • NFR-2: Availability & Fault Tolerance: The platform shall maintain 99.9% uptime. Cloud API rate limits (HTTP 429) or timeouts must trigger automatic fallback without raising unhandled 500 errors.
  • NFR-3: Security & Access Control: Protected endpoints require valid JWT Bearer tokens signed via HMAC-SHA256 (HS256). Enforce 60 requests/minute rate limiting per IP. Inject security headers (X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff, X-Frame-Options: DENY, X-XSS-Protection).

  • NFR-5: Usability & Ergonomics: Single-page dark theme interface optimized for continuous control room monitoring. Interactive elements respond in under 100 ms.
  • NFR-6: Portability: Containerized via Docker and Docker Compose for cross-platform execution on Linux and Windows.

1. Relational Database Schema (PostgreSQL 16 / Supabase)

-- 1. CAMERAS TABLE
CREATE TABLE cameras (
    id VARCHAR(64) PRIMARY KEY,
    name VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,
    location VARCHAR(255) DEFAULT 'Main Area',
    status VARCHAR(32) DEFAULT 'active',
    stream_url TEXT NULL,
    created_at TIMESTAMPTZ DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);

-- 2. EVENTS TABLE (Temporal Frame Analysis Records)
CREATE TABLE events (
    id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
    camera_id VARCHAR(64) REFERENCES cameras(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
    event_time VARCHAR(32) NOT NULL,
    activity TEXT NOT NULL,
    people_count INT DEFAULT 0,
    anomaly_score INT DEFAULT 0 CHECK (anomaly_score BETWEEN 0 AND 10),
    anomaly_reason TEXT NULL,
    objects JSONB DEFAULT '[]'::jsonb,
    location_zone VARCHAR(128) DEFAULT 'unknown',
    faces_visible BOOLEAN DEFAULT FALSE,
    raw_json JSONB NOT NULL,
    created_at TIMESTAMPTZ DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);

-- 3. TRANSCRIPTS TABLE (Synthesized Narrative Shift Logs)
CREATE TABLE transcripts (
    id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
    camera_id VARCHAR(64) REFERENCES cameras(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
    camera_name VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,
    period_start VARCHAR(32) NOT NULL,
    period_end VARCHAR(32) NOT NULL,
    narrative TEXT NOT NULL,
    event_count INT DEFAULT 0,
    events JSONB DEFAULT '[]'::jsonb,
    created_at TIMESTAMPTZ DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);

-- 4. ALERTS TABLE (Security Anomalies where score > 6)
CREATE TABLE alerts (
    id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
    camera_id VARCHAR(64) REFERENCES cameras(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
    camera_name VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,
    event_id UUID REFERENCES events(id) ON DELETE SET NULL,
    time VARCHAR(32) NOT NULL,
    activity TEXT NOT NULL,
    anomaly_score INT NOT NULL,
    anomaly_reason TEXT NOT NULL,
    resolved BOOLEAN DEFAULT FALSE,
    created_at TIMESTAMPTZ DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);

  • Collection: visualiq_transcripts
  • Dimensions: 384 dimensions (normalized floating-point projection)
  • Distance Metric: Cosine similarity ({"hnsw:space": "cosine"})
  • Metadata Fields: camera_id, period, timestamp

3. Redis Key Schema

  • job:{job_id}: JSON tracking status (pending, extracting, analyzing, synthesizing, done, failed) with 1-hour TTL.

4. Methodology Chapter 4

4.1 Modules

The VisualIQ software architecture is partitioned into seven decoupled, cohesive modules:

  1. Video Ingestion & Decimation Engine (services/ingestion.py): Ingests video binaries or consumes RTSP streams. Implements dual-sampling combining uniform decimation (0.2 FPS) with dynamic OpenCV pixel-difference motion detection.
  2. Temporal Context Vision Analyzer (services/analyzer.py): Implements the multimodal vision analysis interface. Injects a sliding window of the previous three frame events into the VLM prompt, enabling accurate loitering detection while maintaining DPDP Act 2023 compliance.
  3. Narrative Shift Synthesizer (services/synthesizer.py): Aggregates frame events across shift intervals (Morning, Afternoon, Night) into professional 2-to-4 sentence narrative surveillance logs.
  4. Vector Embedding & Storage Manager (db/chromadb_client.py): Projects text into 384-dimensional dense vectors and manages HNSW cosine index storage in ChromaDB.
  5. Conversational RAG Engine (services/rag.py): Manages Top-5 vector context retrieval, prompt assembly, and multimodal fallback execution to provide natural language answers with timestamp citations in <3s.
  6. Anomaly Alert & Resolution Manager (routers/alerts.py): Flags events scoring >6 on the threat index, persists alert records, and provides operator resolution workflows.
  7. Web Dashboard & Operator UI (frontend/src/): Single-page React 19 application providing KPI cards, real-time alert panels, chronological timelines, multi-camera filters, and conversational RAG chat.

4.2 Methodology Proposed For Solution Implementation

4.2.1 Mathematical Formulation of Dual-Mode Frame Extraction

Let a video stream $V$ of duration $T$ seconds be represented as a continuous sequence of frames:

V = { ft | t ∈ [0, T] }

1. Uniform Temporal Decimation:
Frames are sampled at fixed intervals:

Δtuniform = 1 / FPSsample = 1 / 0.2 = 5.0 seconds
Equation 4.1: Uniform temporal sampling decimation

2. Motion-Burst Triggering:
Consecutive frames are converted to grayscale and normalized. Let $I_t(x, y) ∈ [0, 1]$ denote normalized luminance at pixel coordinates $(x, y)$ of dimension $W × H$. The global motion delta $M(t)$ is defined as:

M(t) = (1 / (W × H)) ∑x=1W ∑y=1H | It(x, y) - It-δt(x, y) |
Equation 4.2: Normalized inter-frame luminance difference

A motion event is triggered when:

M(t) ≥ θmotion,   where θmotion = 0.08 (8% pixel delta)

Upon trigger activation at timestamp $t^*$, the system extracts a burst sequence $S_{ ext{burst}}$ of $N_{ ext{burst}} \le 5$ frames. The total frame set analyzed is:

Stotal = Suniform ∪ (∪t* Sburst(t*))

This reduces processed frame volume by approximately 96% while ensuring critical transient movements are preserved.

4.2.2 Temporal Context Sliding Window Algorithm

When analyzing frame $f_k$, a sliding historical context window $W_k$ of length $L=3$ is constructed from preceding event logs:

Wk = [ Ek-3, Ek-2, Ek-1 ]

The complete inference prompt $P_k$ provided to the VLM is formulated as:

Pk = Pbase ⊕ Fcontext(Wk) ⊕ Iprivacy

where $F_{ ext{context}}(W_k)$ formats the previous three activities and anomaly scores, and $I_{ ext{privacy}}$ strictly mandates non-biometric, non-facial analysis under the DPDP Act 2023. If a person or vehicle remains in the same zone across consecutive frames:

Δt = tk - tk-3 > 5 minutes &implies; anomaly_score ≥ 7 (Loitering Alert)

4.2.3 Four-Tier Model Cascade & Fallback Execution

To guarantee zero system crashes, VisualIQ routes inference through a four-tier fallback hierarchy:

  1. Tier 1 — Google Gemini 2.5 Flash API: High-precision multimodal model returning structured JSON responses in under 800 ms.
  2. Tier 2 — Groq Cloud (Llama 4 Scout / Llama 3.2 Vision): Ultra-fast LPU inference that automatically engages upon primary API rate limits (HTTP 429).
  3. Tier 3 — Local GPU Ollama (Qwen3-VL 8B): Local edge inference tier capable of operating during complete external network disconnects.
  4. Tier 4 — Deterministic Rule-Based Heuristic: Lightweight non-LLM synthesis engine utilizing regex keyword extraction and template generation. Guarantees 100% demo uptime with zero unhandled exceptions.

4.2.4 Vector Projection & Cosine Similarity Formulation

Surveillance narratives are projected into a 384-dimensional dense vector space. A deterministic projection maps words into coordinate indices $h(w_i) = | ext{Hash}(w_i)| \pmod{384}$, and the resulting vector is $L_2$-normalized:

v̂ = v / ||v||2 = v / √(∑k=1384 v[k]2)
Equation 4.3: L2 unit normalization

Cosine similarity between query embedding $q̂$ and stored document vector $d̂$ is computed as:

Sim(q̂, d̂) = q̂ · d̂ = ∑k=1384 q̂[k] · d̂[k]
Equation 4.4: Cosine similarity in inner product space

ChromaDB uses HNSW graph indexing to retrieve the Top-5 nearest excerpts in $\mathcal{O}(\log M)$ time, supplying the downstream RAG synthesizer with precise evidentiary context.
